Why the sudden scale

AI training requires exponentially more computing power than previous cloud workloads — pushing individual project sizes from hundreds of millions into the tens of billions of dollars.

Why traditional lenders alone can't fund it

No single bank wants this much exposure to one sector — hence the shift toward tranched SPVs, third-party guarantees, and pooled infrastructure funds seen throughout these 10 deals.

Before the deals — 90-second refresher

Three ideas that explain every single deal below

SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle): a separate legal entity created to own one project. It borrows the debt and owns the building — not the parent company. If the project fails, lenders can only claim the SPV's own assets.
The capital stack ("waterfall"): money is raised in layers, safest first — senior debt, then mezzanine debt, then preferred equity, then common equity. Rental income repays these layers in that same order every month.
The one rule that ranks every deal: the less certain it is that a paying customer will occupy the building, the more expensive the financing becomes. This is the strongest predictor of cost of capital across all 10 deals.

DEAL 01

PowerHouse Data Centers → leased to CyrusOne

📍 Ashburn, Virginia, USA — the world's largest data center hub
🏗️ SPECULATIVE SHELL BUILD
PowerHouse Data Centers
A joint venture between American Real Estate Partners (a real estate developer) and Harrison Street (an alternative investment firm focused on real assets), building data center campuses.
CyrusOne
A major global data center operator leasing space to large technology companies; now privately owned by investment firms KKR and Global Infrastructure Partners.

The financing question this answers: can you build an empty building on spec and still get financed? PowerHouse built a "powered shell" — a finished building with electricity connected but no servers installed — with no tenant lined up. CyrusOne later signed a 30-year lease for the entire building.

Money flow
STRUCTUREReal estate joint venture — not a securitized SPV, no tranching
WHY IT WORKEDAshburn's data center market is so deep that a speculative shell was a reasonable bet — demand in that specific location is almost guaranteed eventually
MODERATE RISK — construction risk only, minimal demand risk in this market
DEAL 02

Applied Digital + Macquarie Asset Management

📍 Ellendale, North Dakota, USA
⏳ INVESTMENT BEFORE CONTRACT
Applied Digital
A US-listed company building data centers designed specifically for high-performance computing and AI workloads.
Macquarie Asset Management
The asset management arm of Macquarie Group, a large Australian financial services and infrastructure investment firm.

The financing question this answers: will anyone invest before a tenant signs? Macquarie committed up to $900 million (with rights to a further $4.1 billion) into Applied Digital's campus while it was still unleased. The bet paid off: CoreWeave, a major AI cloud company, later leased the entire 400-megawatt site.

Money flow
STRUCTUREA dedicated holding company issuing perpetual preferred equity plus common equity units
PAYOFFA steady preferred dividend, plus a 15% ownership stake in the entire HPC business, not just this one site
WHY IT COST MOREPure demand risk — no signed tenant at the time of investment, so Macquarie priced in a higher return to compensate
HIGHER RISK — capital committed ahead of any signed lease
DEAL 03

Nvidia's $500 billion financing platforms

📍 Global — not tied to a single site
🤝 INFRA PARTNERSHIP
Nvidia
The world's leading designer of AI computer chips (GPUs), which power most major AI data centers globally.
Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, KKR
Six of the world's largest alternative asset management and investment banking firms.

The financing question this answers: can a chip supplier mobilize investor capital at massive scale without lending the money itself? Rather than financing directly, Nvidia signed agreements with six major institutions to build independent platforms aimed at channeling over $500 billion of third-party capital into Nvidia-based AI infrastructure.

Money flow
STRUCTUREA family of separate financing platforms — not one single fund
STATUSFramework agreements signed in August 2026; detailed execution still in progress
EARLY STAGE — announced framework, not yet fully executed
DEAL 04

Microsoft's AI Infrastructure Partnership (formerly GAIIP)

📍 Multiple US sites; acquired Aligned Data Centers nationally
🤝 INFRA PARTNERSHIP
Microsoft
The technology company behind Azure cloud computing, and a major buyer of AI data center capacity.
BlackRock/GIP, MGX, Kuwait Investment Authority, Temasek
BlackRock is the world's largest asset manager; MGX is an Abu Dhabi state-backed AI investment fund; the others are the sovereign wealth funds of Kuwait and Singapore.

The financing question this answers: can a dedicated fund actually go out and buy existing data center capacity? Launched in September 2024 to fund new data centers and the power infrastructure they require, this partnership has since grown to include Nvidia and xAI, and used its capital to acquire Aligned Data Centers — an established US operator — for roughly $40 billion.

Money flow
STRUCTUREA standalone investment fund that directly owns data center assets
PAYOFFStandard fund returns to investors from owning and leasing the underlying data centers and power assets
MODERATE RISK — diversified across many assets, some already operating
DEAL 05

Google + Blackstone TPU Cloud Joint Venture

📍 United States
🤝 OPERATING JV
Google (Alphabet)
Designs its own AI chips, called TPUs (Tensor Processing Units), as an alternative to Nvidia's GPUs.
Blackstone
The world's largest alternative asset manager, with major real estate and infrastructure investment arms.

The financing question this answers: what does a genuine 50/50 operating company look like, rather than just a financing wrapper? Blackstone contributes $5 billion in cash; Google contributes its TPU chips and supporting software. The joint venture then rents out computing capacity to outside businesses as an ongoing business.

Money flow
STRUCTUREA real 50/50-style operating joint venture with its own management and customers
PAYOFFBlackstone holds majority ownership and profits as the venture sells compute capacity to enterprise customers
MODERATE RISK — new venture, but backed by proven, in-demand technology
DEAL 06

Apollo & Blackstone → Anthropic (Google chip lease)

📍 New York, Texas, Louisiana, and Indiana, USA
🤝 TRANCHED SPV, BACKSTOPPED
Anthropic
An AI research and product company (maker of the Claude AI models) requiring large amounts of computing capacity.
Apollo, Blackstone, Broadcom
Apollo and Blackstone are large alternative investment firms; Broadcom is a semiconductor company that co-designs Google's AI chips and provides a financial guarantee here.

The financing question this answers: how do you finance $36 billion of chip purchases for a single AI company? The vehicle buys Google's chips outright and leases them to Anthropic. Broadcom guarantees the largest tranches — if Anthropic can't pay and the chips can't be resold for enough, Broadcom covers the shortfall. This guarantee is what made a deal this large possible at all.

Money flow
Capital stack — $36B total, in three tranches
LOWER RISK FOR LENDERS — backed by a third-party guarantee
DEAL 07

AdaniConneX debt financing

📍 Chennai, Navi Mumbai, Noida, Vizag, Hyderabad — India
🤝 JV BANK DEBT
Adani Enterprises
A large Indian conglomerate with businesses spanning ports, energy, and infrastructure.
EdgeConneX
A US-headquartered global data center developer and operator.

The financing question this answers: how does an established joint venture raise growth capital once it has a track record? Adani and EdgeConneX formed a 50:50 joint venture in 2021 to build data centers across India. By 2024, with sites already under development, the venture secured a $1.44 billion green loan from a syndicate of eight international banks.

Money flow
STRUCTUREStandard corporate/joint-venture-level bank debt, not a securitization
PAYOFFBank loans repaid from rental income as sites fill with hyperscale and enterprise tenants
MODERATE RISK — an established, proven joint venture with a track record
DEAL 08

Meta + Blue Owl ("Hyperion")

📍 Richland Parish, Louisiana, USA
🤝 20/80 JV, TRANCHED BONDS
Meta
The parent company of Facebook and Instagram, building large-scale data centers to train its AI models.
Blue Owl Capital, PIMCO
Blue Owl is a US alternative asset manager specializing in private credit; PIMCO is one of the world's largest bond investors and purchased most of the debt here.

The financing question this answers: how far can a company go to keep debt off its own balance sheet? Meta owns only 20% of the venture building its own $30 billion campus; Blue Owl-managed funds own the remaining 80%. Meta's own auditor flagged this structure as a "critical audit matter" — meaning it drew unusually close scrutiny.

Money flow
Capital stack — $30B total
FLAGGED BY AUDITOR — an aggressive, closely watched structure
DEAL 09

Lambda — two loans, six months apart

📍 United States
☁️ NEOCLOUD — BEFORE/AFTER
Lambda
A US "neocloud" — a company that rents out GPU computing power specifically for AI workloads, competing with the AI divisions of larger cloud providers.

The financing question this answers: how much does a signed customer actually move the needle? In May 2026, Lambda raised a $1 billion loan with no specific customer attached. Six months later, it raised a second loan secured directly against one already-signed, investment-grade customer's payments — and it was noticeably cheaper.

Money flow — two separate loans compared
MAY LOAN$1B general-purpose syndicated facility, led by JPMorgan, no named customer
AUGUST LOAN$926M asset-backed loan tied to one customer's contracted payments, rated investment-grade
A DIRECT, CLEAN BEFORE/AFTER COMPARISON
DEAL 10

Oracle's Stargate financing

📍 Texas, Wisconsin, and New Mexico, USA
☁️ MINI-PERM REFINANCING BET
Oracle
A major enterprise software and cloud infrastructure company, financing sites connected to the large-scale "Stargate" AI infrastructure initiative.

The financing question this answers: can you borrow short-term and bet on cheaper refinancing later? Oracle raised roughly $38 billion and $18 billion in four-year loans, deliberately choosing a short initial term, betting it can refinance into cheaper, longer-term debt once these buildings are stabilized.

Money flow
STRUCTUREFour-year "mini-perm" loans, priced roughly 2.5 percentage points above the risk-free government borrowing rate
THE RISKThe entire bet depends on favorable refinancing conditions being available in four years — if borrowing costs rise instead, Oracle could be stuck with more expensive debt
A DELIBERATE BET ON FUTURE MARKET CONDITIONS

Quick reference

Financing terms used across these deals

SPV
A separate legal entity created to own one project, isolating its risk from the parent company.
Tranche
A risk-ranked slice of debt — safer tranches are repaid first, at a lower rate.
Backstop / guarantee
A third party's promise to cover losses if the primary borrower cannot pay — lowers risk for lenders.
Mini-perm loan
Short-term financing (often 3-5 years) taken with the intention of refinancing once the asset stabilizes.
Cost of capital
The return investors demand for taking on a deal's risk — higher for riskier, less certain deals.
Residual value guarantee
A promise to cover the gap if an asset is worth less than expected when a lease ends.
